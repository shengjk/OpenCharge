# Quit and discharge recovery

Ordinary monitoring-only quit remains immediate. When the app owns charge, sleep-assertion or LED work, Quit first prepares that session for exit. Policy refreshes and new in-app hardware changes pause during preparation. The existing quit setting still decides whether charging is restored or inhibited after exit; that inhibition request also releases active adapter discharge. LED restoration and the app's idle-sleep assertion are cleaned up independently, with errors reported.

After this app has successfully requested active discharge, a successful helper response alone is insufficient. The app waits for the OS to report external power, or battery power with an explicitly absent physical adapter. Safe observations must remain stable for at least half a second, with no sampling gap longer than one second. Longer gaps reset that confirmation. Missing properties, unknown sources and continued battery use with a connected/unknown adapter cannot prove recovery. Time uses system uptime rather than calendar time.

The wait lasts at most 15 seconds before asking for a decision. Cleanup failures also ask for a decision. Polling stops while that decision is pending and restarts only on retry. **Retry** repeats cleanup and starts a fresh wait. **Cancel Quit** keeps the app open and disables charge control; it does not automatically resume discharge or calibration. Calibration requires explicit continuation. Closing the quit window also cancels. **Exit Anyway** is offered only after failure/timeout and may leave recovery unconfirmed; a brief display interruption is possible and the helper may still need its heartbeat timeout to release a non-retained session. Cancel Quit now attempts to restore ordinary charge state, including retained inhibition requested during preparation. A failed or ambiguously acknowledged restore keeps process-local recovery responsibility and the next quit retries it; re-enabling control also requires restoration first. A clean cancellation does not repeat hardware cleanup. These acknowledgements do not prove physical adapter/display recovery. Numeric firmware exit is different: its existing helper command commits a target and relinquishes its journal, so an ordinary restore acknowledgement after that commit cannot prove the target was undone. A cancellable numeric exit transaction and crash/restart recovery remain open work; the app does not overwrite that target by guessing an unrestricted value.

This uses [AppKit deferred termination and its required reply](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsapplication/reply%28toapplicationshouldterminate%3A%29). OS power-source confirmation uses [IOPSGetProvidingPowerSourceType](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/iokit/iopowersources_h/1810316-iopsgetprovidingpowersourcetype); a physically connected adapter is distinct from the source currently powering the computer. The helper's fixed commands and watchdog remain unchanged.

A native fake-only harness has exercised real AppKit delayed termination and its retry/cancel/explicit-exit choices. Real charging writes, display transitions, actual sleep/logout/shutdown, external controllers and forced termination remain unverified. Force Quit and power loss cannot run this application-level cleanup. See [verification](TESTING.md) and [status](STATUS.md).
